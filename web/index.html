“Cut our AI bill” is three numbers, not one

Cost, answer quality and speed move together, so every saving is a trade. I tested configurations of a question answering and summarisation pipeline and used Bayesian optimisation to map the trade-offs in 20 paid runs.

How this was measured

Quality is the share of 60 requests (30 questions, 30 news summaries) that got a complete answer which a hallucination judge found faithful to the source. The judge is Claude Haiku 5.5, checked against RAGTruth human labels (Cohen’s kappa 0.63).

Cost is generation cost per 1,000 requests at list prices. Speed is the 95th percentile response time. Five settings were varied: model, prompt, how much reference material is kept, answer length limit and reasoning effort, giving 108 configurations.

Search. One Gaussian process per objective and expected hypervolume improvement (BoTorch) chose each next run from all untested configurations.

What went wrong first. My first result looked too good: answers cut off at the length limit, and quotes copied from the source, counted as faithful. Hidden reasoning tokens were using up the answer budget. Quality now requires a complete answer.

Limits. With 60 requests, quality figures carry about ±8 points of uncertainty. The shared starting set never tried a 512 token limit or low effort, which slowed the Bayesian search early on. The human labels date from 2023 era models.
